Transforming Filling Machinery in the Cosmetics Industry

In recent weeks, filling and packaging equipment manufacturers at beauty industry trade shows and specialist media have clearly shifted their focus from 'production line speed' to 'production line flexibility.' The reason is simple: today's cosmetics brands work with more diverse product baskets, smaller batch sizes and faster design cycles than ever before, and lines optimized only for high volume and a single formula can no longer serve this business model.

Recent reports show that major manufacturers' investment in modular filling lines has increased — lines that can switch between different viscosities and packaging sizes within hours instead of days. Inline quality control — measuring weight, volume and seal of each unit in real time with sensors instead of periodic sampling — has become an unwritten standard on new lines.

Another highlight from these reports is the growing demand for filling machines compatible with sensitive formulas (such as thick serums or formulas containing probiotics and natural oils) that require more precise pressure and temperature control during filling — exactly the kind of issue that leads to leakage, foaming or line blockage when the pump and discharge mechanism aren't compatible with the formula.

Source: Packaging Digest

New 3 Sep 2026 · Week 34

Return of Glass & Metal: New Wave of Sustainable & Anti-Counterfeit Packaging

One of the most prominent packaging trends in recent months is the clear return of premium cosmetics brands to glass and metal materials instead of single-use plastics. This move is driven both by EU regulatory pressure on recyclable packaging and by shifting consumer sentiment that equates the 'weight and tactile quality' of packaging with product value.

Alongside this trend, a second fast-growing area is smart anti-counterfeit packaging. Major cosmetics brands are increasingly using unique QR codes, security holograms and even NFC tags on packaging so consumers can verify product authenticity before purchase — especially in online sales and export markets. This technology has seen significant growth in markets like the Middle East and Southeast Asia, where counterfeit cosmetics are a serious concern for brands.

A key takeaway for plastic parts manufacturers is that this shift doesn't mean eliminating plastic — it means 'smarter plastic': pumps and caps that are dimensionally compatible with glass or metal bodies, and can incorporate security features (like anti-counterfeit rings or tamper-evident seals) into their design.

Source: Global Cosmetics News

Artificial Intelligence: The New Wave in Beauty

AI is no longer a fringe experiment in the beauty industry. Giants like L'Oréal and Unilever have announced new investments in AI tools for skin analysis, personalized formula recommendations and even designing new active ingredient molecules. These tools have reduced the process that previously took months — from idea to prototype formula — to just a few weeks.

In the sales and customer experience space, retailers like Target have also stepped up: launching ChatGPT-powered stores that help customers find the right cosmetics based on skin type, budget and preferences — a trend that was also a major focus at CES 2026 beauty booths.

For the beauty industry as a whole, this wave means a shorter gap between idea and final product — brands can move from data analysis to new formulas faster than ever before.

Five Raw Material Trends Shaping Future Formulations

Recent specialist formulation reports have identified five key directions in cosmetics raw materials that will likely shape next year's products:

1. Longevity: Products marketed using the language of cellular longevity science and deep anti-aging, going beyond surface-level wrinkle reduction.

2. Scalp Care as an independent branch of hair care, with specialized serum-like formulas.

3. Multifunctional products that combine multiple beauty routine steps into one — addressing consumer time scarcity.

4. Natural alternatives to microneedling, including sea sponge extracts that simulate collagen stimulation without needles.

5. Natural Gelling Agents instead of synthetic polymers, for 'clean' beauty formulas.

What these five trends have in common is that each changes the viscosity, texture or flow behavior of a formula during filling and pumping — thicker serums, natural gels with different flow behavior from synthetic polymers, and multifunctional formulas that are usually thicker than a simple lotion.

Glass Airless Pump 'Celebrity': Combining Glass Luxury with Airless Technology

At the MakeUp in Paris exhibition (June 2026), Italian company Mktg Industry unveiled a new packaging system called "Celebrity" — the company's first airless pump with a glass body. It is available in three capacities (15, 30 and 50 ml) and is designed for premium skin care products and serums.

The distinguishing feature of Celebrity is that it reconciles two seemingly contradictory demands of the cosmetic packaging industry: the beauty and premium feel of glass on one hand, and the efficiency of airless technology on the other. Airless systems prevent air from entering the container, protecting sensitive formulations such as vitamin C and retinol from oxidation, enable full evacuation of the product and reduce microbial contamination. Until now, most airless pumps were produced with plastic bodies so that the internal pressurisation mechanism would work properly; achieving the same performance with a glass body is a genuine engineering challenge.

The product is also notable from an environmental perspective: all its plastic components — apart from the glass itself — are made from a single polymer (polypropylene), and no metal parts are used in the pump structure, a feature that makes recycling considerably easier. The refills and caps are also fully mono-material, and consumers can place an empty refill directly into the polypropylene recycling stream. The design comes as the European Union's Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) approaches its full application on 12 August 2026.

This example shows that the future of premium packaging does not necessarily pass through the total replacement of materials, but rather through a clever combination of traditional materials (glass) with functional technologies and mono-material design for better recyclability. In the coming months, other pump manufacturers are expected to introduce similar models for premium and anti-ageing product lines.

New 12 Sep 2026

EU Packaging Regulation (PPWR) Takes Full Effect on 12 August 2026

The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR, No. 2025/40) takes full effect on 12 August 2026, with many of its requirements leaving no transitional period. It is one of the most comprehensive regulatory frameworks for the European packaging industry in the past decade and directly affects the cosmetics and personal care industry, since nearly every product in this sector — from creams and serums to fragrance and shampoo — is sold in some form of packaging.

Among the most important requirements that apply from day one are limits on the presence of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in food-contact packaging and the obligation to minimise "substances of concern" in all packaging types, including cosmetics packaging. In addition, every packaging format must have an official EU Declaration of Conformity with supporting technical documentation; manufacturers must also design packaging so that it can be identified and traced.

Another important horizon is 2030, when restrictions will apply to certain single-use plastic formats — including small miniature packaging commonly used in the hospitality industry and cosmetic sample products. Packaging experts recommend that brands and manufacturers start now and allow 12 to 24 months to redesign packaging and secure recycled materials, so they are ready well before the 2030 deadlines arrive.

The importance of this regulation extends well beyond the EU; any brand or manufacturer planning to export to this market, or working with European brands, must align its packaging supply chain with these standards. Overall, this trend shows that the global packaging industry — regardless of where manufacturers are located — is moving toward stricter documentation requirements and mono-material design for recyclability.

Source: Obelis / CIRS Group

New 12 Sep 2026

2026 Cosmetic Packaging Outlook: From Sensory Appeal to Sustainability Transparency

New analyses of cosmetic packaging trends for 2026 reveal several parallel and sometimes contrasting paths that together paint a clear picture of consumer and industry demands. The first trend is "sensorial packaging": designs built for sharing on social media and for satisfying tactile experiences — from soft-touch finishes to pump mechanisms with a distinctive sound and feel that help sell on platforms like TikTok Shop.

Alongside this, the move toward "mono-material" pump components and airless systems has accelerated. Pumps and caps made entirely from a single polymer (only polypropylene or only polyethylene) or from glass and paper solve the historic challenge of multi-material packaging that is difficult to recycle. Aluminium is also establishing a stronger foothold in tubes, cans, pots and bottles since 2025, with brands like Dieux, Experiment Beauty and Grown Alchemist among its pioneers.

The third trend is a shift in sustainability discourse from slogans to measurable transparency. Merely claiming "recyclability" is no longer enough; brands must provide data and evidence. A striking example is the Radox brand, which deliberately launched bottles in darker tones (thanks to 50% recycled plastic) to showcase the transparency of its production process rather than hide it. Smart technologies such as QR codes, NFC and RFID are also becoming standard tools for linking the physical product to recycling information, authenticity checks and subscription schemes.

Finally, portable and wearable formats are also growing — from lipsticks embedded in necklace pendants to keychain blushes and multi-purpose stick products. These trends together show that cosmetic packaging is no longer merely a container for the product, but a platform for sensory experience, proof of environmental responsibility and digital engagement with the consumer.

2026 Wave of M&A in the Global Beauty and Personal Care Industry

2026 has been one of the most dynamic years for mergers and acquisitions (M&A) in the beauty industry and its packaging. One of the largest deals is the acquisition of the specialist hair care brand Olaplex by chemical-consumer giant Henkel, at a value close to $1.4 billion — a deal aimed at strengthening Henkel's position in premium hair care and adding the brand's innovation capacity to the company's broader portfolio.

At the luxury brand level, WHP Group acquired the Marc Jacobs fashion brand from LVMH, while the creative continuity of the brand is preserved with the founder-designer staying on. Estée Lauder Companies also acquired the remaining stake in the Indian Ayurvedic brand Forest Essentials after an 18-year partnership — a sign of major Western companies' growing interest in regional brands with strong cultural roots in emerging markets. Investment fund KKR also consolidated its presence in East Asia by buying the Japanese beauty platform Ci Flavors (including the brands &honey, 8 The Thalasso and unlabel).

A point of particular relevance for the industry's supply chain is that the M&A wave is not limited to consumer brands; several strategic deals have also been recorded among suppliers and manufacturers of packaging components. For instance, Cosmogen and Asquan merged to form the joint group "Cosmogen & Asquan" to expand packaging services, and Truvant acquired Multi-Pak Packaging to extend its activity into vitamin and supplement packaging.

Overall, this trend shows that the global beauty market is consolidating around a few larger, more diversified players, while the supply chain (including packaging component manufacturers) is also undergoing mergers to gain scale, product diversity and geographic coverage. For smaller and regional players, these developments can be both an opportunity to collaborate with larger players and a wake-up call to sharpen competitive differentiation.

New 12 Sep 2026

Middle East Beauty Market to Reach $20.8 Billion by 2030

According to a recent report published in the trade magazine HPC Middle East & Africa, the Middle East's beauty and personal care market will grow from $14.3 billion in 2025 to $20.8 billion by 2030. In 2025 the region grew by 16% — far faster than the global beauty market's 6% average.

The main engine of this growth is strong demand from the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries — Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. A young, digitally connected population (more than 50% under 30), rising disposable income, urbanisation and a growing preference for premium products are the key drivers of this boom. Saudi Arabia alone accounts for about 40% of GCC beauty spending, with average per-capita spending reaching $164.9.

Among product categories, fragrance remains the most attractive segment of the market; fragrance consumption in Saudi Arabia is about four times the European or US average. Skin care is also emerging as the next growth frontier, especially among Gen Z consumers who closely follow global beauty trends.

The report also notes challenges in the region's supply chain in early 2026 stemming from geopolitical tensions, which caused shortages of some raw materials for certain manufacturers; in response, companies have adopted strategies such as increasing financial support to suppliers and diversifying supply sources. This point highlights, more than ever, the importance of reliable regional suppliers — instead of complete dependence on long-distance imports.

The growth of the Middle East market, especially in fragrance and skin care, directly translates into rising demand for packaging, spray pumps and lotion pumps in the region — a market growing faster than the global average, offering significant opportunities for packaging component manufacturers close to the region.

Source: HPC Magazine Middle East & Africa

New 12 Sep 2026

Cosmetic Packaging Machinery Market Moves Toward Smart Automation

A new report on the global cosmetic packaging machinery market shows that this market, worth $4.2 billion in 2025, will reach $7.3 billion by 2035, with a compound annual growth rate of 5.6%. Within this, the filling machinery segment alone generated $1.2 billion in 2025.

The main engine of this growth is the expansion of automation and "Industry 4.0" concepts in cosmetics and personal care production lines. Machinery manufacturers are increasingly adopting "smart manufacturing" solutions that use digital technologies to enable equipment monitoring and production process optimisation. Rising global cosmetics consumption on one hand, and labour cost pressure on the other, are pushing manufacturers toward more advanced systems offering higher output, greater precision and more consistent performance.

An interesting point in the report is that despite media focus on full automation, semi-automatic equipment still accounts for 54.8% of the market and remains the dominant segment. This equipment is especially attractive to small and medium manufacturers, offering a cost-effective solution with high operational flexibility — in contrast to fully automatic systems designed for large-scale production. The US market alone generated $1.3 billion in 2025 and is expanding at 5.3%, a trend driven mainly by strict quality requirements and an emphasis on sustainability and innovation in this market.

Major players in this industry include the IMA, Coesia, Marchesini, OPTIMA Packaging and Barry-Wehmiller groups, which are strengthening their competitive positions through investment in R&D, strategic partnerships and smart-factory solutions.

This transformation also carries a significant implication for packaging component manufacturers: aligning the geometry and tolerances of pumps with the standards of modern semi-automatic and automatic filling lines is no longer an optional competitive advantage, but a core prerequisite for collaborating with brands and manufacturers equipped with these modern systems.

Source: GlobeNewswire (Cosmetic Packaging Machinery Market Analysis)
