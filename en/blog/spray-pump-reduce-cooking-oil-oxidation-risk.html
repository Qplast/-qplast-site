How Can a Spray Pump Help Reduce the Risk of Cooking Oil Oxidation?

Contact with air: where quality loss begins

When cooking oil is in continuous contact with air, it gradually oxidises, a process that can reduce its flavour, colour and quality. The more open the path for air into the package and the more frequent its contact with the oil, the higher this risk. For cooking oil producers and suppliers, package design is one factor that can help manage this risk while the end customer uses the product. Besides air, light and heat also affect how fast oil oxidises (for more on the role of light and a matte body, read the article "Why Is the Body of a Cooking Oil Spray Matte?" on the Q Plast blog).

Capped bottle versus spray pump

With ordinary bottles, each time the cap is opened for pouring, a relatively large opening is exposed to ambient air, and if the cap is not closed right away, that exposure continues. With a spray pump, oil leaves only through the pump's narrow channel and only at the moment of pressing, with no need to open a cap. This difference can help reduce how often and how long the oil is in direct contact with outside air between uses, although as oil is used, air gradually takes its place in the package.

Frequently asked questions

Does a cooking oil spray pump completely eliminate oil oxidation?

No. Under normal conditions, the pump package design can only help reduce the risk of oxidation, not eliminate it entirely. Light, heat and the quality of the original oil also play a role.

Does Q Oil Spray have a food-contact certificate?

The food-contact certificate for the finished product has not yet been issued and is under technical review.

What other factors affect oil oxidation in packaging?

Besides how much and how long the oil is in contact with air, light and heat also affect how fast oil oxidises. The metal, matte body of Q Oil Spray is not transparent and can help reduce the light reaching the oil. Keeping the product away from heat sources is also recommended.
